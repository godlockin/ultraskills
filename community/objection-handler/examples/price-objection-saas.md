# Price Objection - SaaS 7-Figure Deal

**Context:**
- Enterprise SaaS deal, $500K ARR
- Competitive against market leader (40% cheaper)
- Champion identified, but CFO pushed back
- MEDDIC score: 7/10 (Metrics + Pain strong, Economic Buyer weak)

**Objection:**
CFO: "Your pricing is $200K more than [Competitor]. That's a 40% premium. We need you to match their price."

---

## Response Breakdown

### 1. Acknowledge (Validate)
"I appreciate the transparent benchmark, and I respect the CFO discipline on pricing — especially with budget scrutiny this year."

**Why this works:**
- Shows you understand their constraints
- Positions price discussion as business decision, not haggling

---

### 2. Reframe (Metrics-driven)

"Let me ask: what's driving the evaluation — lowest cost, or highest ROI?

Here's the math our customers run:

**Current state pain** (from discovery):
- 15% annual customer churn
- $8M ARR base
- **Churn cost: $1.2M/year**

**[Competitor] impact** (per their case studies):
- Average churn reduction: 3 percentage points
- **Value: $240K/year saved**

**Our impact** (per 12 similar customers):
- Average churn reduction: 8 percentage points
- **Value: $640K/year saved**

**Price delta analysis:**
- Our price: $500K
- [Competitor] price: $300K
- **Delta: $200K**

**ROI comparison:**
| Solution | Year 1 Value | Price | Net Benefit | ROI |
|----------|--------------|-------|-------------|-----|
| [Competitor] | $240K | $300K | -$60K | -20% |
| Us | $640K | $500K | **+$140K** | **28%** |

Would you rather save $200K upfront and lose $640K in retained revenue, or invest $200K more for $400K additional value?"

---

### 3. Advance (De-risk decision)

"Two ways to validate this:

**Option 1: Reference calls**
Connect with [Customer X] (same industry, same churn problem). They paid our full price, saw 9-point churn drop in 5 months, and renewed at 120% this year.

**Option 2: Pilot structure**
6-month pilot at $250K (50% discount). If you don't hit 6-point churn reduction, we refund 100% and you keep the software.

Which de-risks this better for the CFO?"

---

## Outcome
- CFO agreed to reference call
- Reference customer shared 8-point churn improvement data
- Deal closed at $475K (5% discount, not 40%)
- Customer hit 7-point churn drop in Q1, renewed early at $550K

**Key lesson:** Price objection = value articulation failure. Fix with metrics + social proof, not discounts.
